The system · Currency AI

Currency AI — a regime detection filter

The core signal system fires alerts across 29 currency pairs on five timeframes — 1 minute through 2 hour — validated against out-of-sample session windows built from 65,000+ historical trades. The system identifies trend continuation setups, not reversals.

The problem Currency AI solves is market regime. In consolidating markets the system performs well. In strongly trending markets — when capital flows are aggressively directional — trend-following signals on the wrong side of that move get destroyed regardless of session window quality. The system needed a way to detect regime in real time and suppress counter-regime signals automatically.

"Each currency is treated as an agent in a network. The 26 forex pairs are edges. The rolling win rate filter is an emergent signal from the collective behaviour of those agents — not a rule I wrote, but a property of the system."

For each currency, the system maintains a rolling 30-trade window tracking performance across all pairs in which that currency appears — both as base and quote, direction-adjusted. GBP's strength is calculated from every trade where GBP was held long: GBPUSD buys, EURGBP sells, GBPJPY buys.

Before any signal reaches a member's screen, two conditions must both be met:

CONDITION 1
Long currency is strong
Win rate when held long must exceed 42.9% (breakeven at 1.4R/R). Below this threshold → signal suppressed.
CONDITION 2
Short currency is weak
The currency being sold must be losing when held long — indicating genuine weakness, not just noise.

Minimum 10 trades of history are required before either condition is evaluated. Below this threshold, the signal is suppressed — not shown. When in doubt, exclude.

In development · The next layer

An immutable trade ledger

The industry standard for signal verification — platforms like MyFXBook — has a well-documented vulnerability. Providers can pause tracking before bad trades and re-enable it after. The record only reflects what they chose to show. This is quietly exploited across the industry.

The current Perfect Entry system already improves on this significantly. Every Currency AI verdict is calculated and stored at the moment a signal opens — before the outcome is known — and cannot be changed after the fact. Every close source is recorded: automated, auto-close engine, or manual. The record is auditable even if not cryptographically sealed.

The next step is a cryptographic hash chain where each block hashes the previous. Any retroactive change breaks the chain from that point forward and is immediately detectable.

Open questions

What I haven't solved yet

  • 01
    What is the optimal lookback window for the currency filter? The current 30-trade window was determined empirically. A formal optimisation over different market regimes would establish whether this is robust or overfitted to the current data period.
  • 02
    Should the weights between session score, currency strength, and pair-level profit factor be learned rather than specified? A logistic regression over 1,000+ trades per bucket would allow the system to discover the empirically optimal weighting.
  • 03
    Is win rate when held long the right proxy for currency strength, or does win rate when held short diverge in ways that encode additional information? Initial data suggests long + short WR does not always sum to 100%, which implies asymmetric information content.
  • 04
    The Monday underperformance pattern is consistent across two weeks of data. Is this a liquidity effect, a regime transition effect from weekend gaps, or something else? How should the system handle the first N hours of each trading week?
  • 05
    If the currency network state is timestamped at every signal event, can extreme divergences between long and short win rates serve as an early warning signal for currency stress events — a real-time stress indicator built from aggregate retail trader behaviour?
